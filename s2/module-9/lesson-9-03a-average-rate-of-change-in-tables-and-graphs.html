📚 VOCABULARY

Average rate of change: The change in the value of a function's output divided by the change in the input over a specific interval.

Function: A relationship where each input has exactly one output.

Input: The value substituted into a function, often represented by \(x\).

Interval: A set of numbers between two endpoints, often describing the domain of inputs being considered.

Output: The result or value produced by a function after substituting an input, often represented by \(f(x)\) or \(y\).

Slope: A measure of the steepness of a line, found by dividing the change in \(y\) by the change in \(x\) between two points.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to find the average rate of change in nonlinear functions. Understanding this concept is essential because, unlike linear functions where the rate of change is constant, nonlinear functions have rates of change that vary across different intervals. By the end of this lesson, you will know how to calculate the average rate of change from a table of values and from a graph, both in mathematical examples and real-world contexts.


Average Rate of Change from a Table of Values

The average rate of change between two points on a function is found by subtracting the function's outputs and dividing by the difference in the inputs.

Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\)

where \(a\) and \(b\) are the inputs or x values and \(f(a)\) and \(f(b)\) are their corresponding outputs or y values.



Example 1

The table of values shown represents a function.

x y
1 2
2 5
3 10
4 17
5 26

Find the average rate of change from \(x=2\) to \(x=5\).

Solution:

  • First we want to find the outputs for \(f(2)\) and \(f(5)\).
  • We will go to 2 in the x column for the input 2 and find the y value of 5 for the output.
  • This means that \(f(2)=5\).
  • Then we go to 5 in the x column and find its output.
  • This means that \(f(5)=26\).
  • Now to find the average rate of change.
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{26-5}{5-2}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{21}{3}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=7\)

The average rate of change from \(x=2\) to \(x=5\) is \(7\).



Example 2

A car's speed, in miles per hour, at different times is recorded in the table, where x is the hours and y is the distance.

Time Distance
0 0
1 50
2 120
3 210
4 320

Find the average rate of change between 1 hour and 3 hours.

Solution:

  • First we want to find the outputs for \(f(1)\) and \(f(3)\).
  • This means that \(f(1)=50\) and \(f(3)=210\).
  • Now to find the average rate of change.
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{210-50}{3-1}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{160}{2}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=80\)

The car's average speed was 80 miles per hour between 1 and 3 hours.



Average Rate of Change from a Graph

When given a graph, the process is similar. Find the coordinates of the two points, then use the same formula for the average rate of change.


Example 3

A graph of a nonlinear function is shown.

Find the average rate of change between \(x=1\) and \(x=4\).

Solution:

  • Let's identify the input and outputs.
  • \(f(1)=1\) and \(f(4)=16\)
  • Now we can find the average rate of change.
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{16-1}{4-1}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{15}{3}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=5\)
  • The average rate of change between \(x=1\) and \(x=4\) is \(5\).



Example 4

The graph shown represents the height (in meters) of a hot air balloon over time (seconds).

Find the average rate of change of the balloon between \(x=2\) and \(x=6\).

Solution:

  • Let's identify the input and outputs.
  • \(f(2)=9.9\) and \(f(6)=29.8\)
  • Now we can find the average rate of change.
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{29.8-9.9}{6-2}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{19.9}{4}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=4.975\)
  • The balloon's average rate of change between \(x=2\) and \(x=6\) seconds is \(4.975\) meters per second.



Conclusion

Today we learned that while nonlinear functions have varying rates of change, we can still find the average rate of change over an interval. Whether using a table of values or a graph, the formula remains the same: change in output divided by change in input. By practicing with mathematical and real-world examples, you are now better prepared to understand how functions behave over specific intervals. Keep practicing, and soon finding average rates of change will become second nature!
