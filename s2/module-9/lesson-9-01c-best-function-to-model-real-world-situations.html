📚 VOCABULARY

Absolute Value Function: A function that measures the distance of a number from zero on the number line, regardless of direction. The graph of an absolute value function typically forms a V shape.

Common Difference: The difference between consecutive terms in an arithmetic sequence. It's a constant value that, when added to a term in the sequence, gives the next term.

Common Ratio: The ratio between consecutive terms in a geometric sequence. It's a constant value that, when multiplied by a term in the sequence, gives the next term.

Cube Root Function: A function of the form \(y=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\), where \(a\neq 0\). Its graph is an S-shaped curve that can extend infinitely in both the positive and negative directions.

Cubic Function: A function that represents the cube root of x, typically written as \(y=\sqrt[3]{x} \)​. Its graph passes through the origin and increases in both the positive and negative directions of x.

Curvature: The rate at which a curve deviates from being a straight line. In algebra, it’s often discussed in the context of quadratic and cubic functions.

Exponential Function: A function where the independent variable appears in the exponent, typically of the form \(y=a(b)^x\), where \(b>0\) and \(b\neq 1\). Its graph shows rapid growth or decay.

Function: A relationship between two sets that assigns exactly one output for each input. It is often represented as \(y=f(x)\).

Growth Rate: The rate at which a quantity increases over time. In the context of exponential functions, it refers to how quickly the function's value increases as x increases.

Linear Function: A function of the form \(y=mx+b\) where \(m\) and \(b\) are constants, and \(m\) is the slope. Its graph is a straight line.

Minimums: The lowest point on the graph of a function. For quadratic functions with a positive leading coefficient, the vertex represents the minimum value.

Maximums: The highest point on the graph of a function. For quadratic functions with a negative leading coefficient, the vertex represents the maximum value.

Quadratic Function: A function of the form \(y=ax^2+bx+c\) where \(a\neq 0\). Its graph is a parabola that can open upwards or downwards.

Sequential: A term used to describe objects (like numbers or functions) that follow a specific order or sequence.

Slope: A measure of the steepness of a line, defined as the ratio of the vertical change to the horizontal change between two points on the line. For linear functions, the slope is the coefficient of \(x\).

Square Root Function: A function that represents the square root of \(x\), written as \(y=\sqrt{x} \)​. Its graph is a half-parabola that opens to the right.

Vertex: The highest or lowest point of a parabola, depending on its direction. The vertex of a quadratic function is a point where the function attains its maximum or minimum value.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to determine if a real-world scenario is best modeled by a linear, quadratic, or exponential function. Understanding the characteristics of each function type is crucial for interpreting and solving problems you may encounter in everyday life or in various academic disciplines. By identifying key features in the description of a scenario, you can select the appropriate function type to represent the situation mathematically. This skill is not only fundamental in Algebra 1 but also forms the basis for more advanced mathematical reasoning and problem-solving. Let's look at what to watch for in a scenario to distinguish which function it represents, followed by specific examples for practice.


Let's begin by watching a video on the three different types of functions we will be exploring and key characteristics to look for in real-world situations to determine which one will best model the given situation.

Characteristics to Distinguish Function Types

  • Linear Functions:

    • Represents constant growth or decay.
    • Involves a scenario where something is added or subtracted at a steady rate.
    • Look for phrases like "increases by a constant amount" or "decreases by the same number" over equal intervals.

  • Quadratic Functions:

    • Models situations involving acceleration or deceleration.
    • Involves scenarios where the rate of change itself changes over time.
    • Look for descriptions of objects being thrown, paths of projectiles, or any situation where growth starts slowly and then speeds up, or vice versa.
    • Heights or distances will increase then decrease or decrease then increase over time showing something going down then back up, or vice versa.

  • Exponential Functions:

    • Used to model rapid growth or decay.
    • Involves scenarios where quantities double, halve, or change by a certain percentage over equal time periods.
    • Key phrases include "doubles every year," "decreases by 20% annually," or "grows exponentially."


Example 1

A taxi company charges a flat rate fee for picking up a passenger and then an additional fixed fee per mile driven.

Which function best models the total charge for a ride based on the miles driven?

Solution:

  • This situation is best modeled by a linear function.
  • Reasoning: The cost of a taxi ride starts with a flat rate fee, which does not change based on the distance traveled. For every mile driven, there's an additional fixed fee. This situation exemplifies a constant rate of change—regardless of how far the passenger travels, the charge per mile remains the same, and the initial charge (the flat rate) is a one-time addition at the beginning of the ride. This pattern of adding a fixed amount to a starting value is characteristic of linear functions, where the graph of the total charge versus miles driven would be a straight line, indicating a uniform rate of addition.


Example 2

A fountain projects water upwards, and the height of the water at various times is measured. The water reaches a peak height before falling back down.

Which function best models the height of the water over time?

Solution:

  • This situation is best modeled by a quadratic function.
  • Reasoning: The movement of water in this fountain, where it is projected upwards and then falls back to the ground, follows a path shaped by gravitational acceleration. This path is parabolic, a hallmark of quadratic functions. Initially, the water speeds up as it moves upward but then slows down after reaching a peak height, before accelerating downwards as it falls back. This acceleration and deceleration, where the rate of height change varies, fits the profile of a quadratic function, which is capable of modeling scenarios where the rate of change itself changes over time. The peak height of the water corresponds to the vertex of the parabola, demonstrating why a quadratic function, with its characteristic parabolic graph, best models this scenario.


Example 3

A population of bacteria in a petri dish doubles every hour under ideal conditions. The initial count of bacteria is known.

Which function best models the growth of the bacteria population over time?

Solution:

  • This situation is best modeled by an exponential function.
  • Reasoning: The population of bacteria doubling every hour signifies a growth pattern where the size of the population at any given time depends multiplicatively on its size at the previous hour. This kind of growth, where a quantity increases by a consistent percentage or factor over equal intervals of time, is indicative of exponential functions. Unlike linear or quadratic growth, exponential growth accelerates at a rate proportional to the current quantity, leading to a rapid increase. This scenario is characterized by a common ratio (the population size doubling, or multiplying by a factor of 2, each hour), which aligns with the defining feature of exponential functions. The graph of this situation would show a steep curve increasing upwards, reflecting the bacteria's explosive growth over time.


** In each scenario, the key lies in identifying the nature of the change or growth presented and matching it with the function type whose characteristics best describe that pattern. Linear functions model scenarios with constant rates of change, quadratic functions fit situations involving acceleration and deceleration, and exponential functions are suited for cases of multiplicative or percentage-based growth or decay.


Conclusion

By examining real-world scenarios for specific indicators, such as the rate of change, acceleration, or proportional growth/decay, we can identify the most appropriate mathematical model to represent the situation. Linear functions are characterized by constant rates of change, quadratic functions by variable rates of change (acceleration), and exponential functions by multiplicative rates of change. Recognizing these features in the description of a scenario allows you to choose between linear, quadratic, and exponential functions for modeling the situation accurately. This approach not only enhances your problem-solving skills but also deepens your understanding of how mathematics is applied in the real world. As you encounter various scenarios, practice identifying these key characteristics to determine the most suitable function type for modeling each situation.
