📚 VOCABULARY

Absolute Value Function: A function that measures the distance of a number from zero on the number line, regardless of direction. The graph of an absolute value function typically forms a V shape.

Common Difference: The difference between consecutive terms in an arithmetic sequence. It's a constant value that, when added to a term in the sequence, gives the next term.

Common Ratio: The ratio between consecutive terms in a geometric sequence. It's a constant value that, when multiplied by a term in the sequence, gives the next term.

Cube Root Function: A function of the form \(y=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\), where \(a\neq 0\). Its graph is an S-shaped curve that can extend infinitely in both the positive and negative directions.

Cubic Function: A function that represents the cube root of x, typically written as \(y=\sqrt[3]{x} \)​. Its graph passes through the origin and increases in both the positive and negative directions of x.

Curvature: The rate at which a curve deviates from being a straight line. In algebra, it’s often discussed in the context of quadratic and cubic functions.

Exponential Function: A function where the independent variable appears in the exponent, typically of the form \(y=a(b)^x\), where \(b>0\) and \(b\neq 1\). Its graph shows rapid growth or decay.

Function: A relationship between two sets that assigns exactly one output for each input. It is often represented as \(y=f(x)\).

Growth Rate: The rate at which a quantity increases over time. In the context of exponential functions, it refers to how quickly the function's value increases as x increases.

Linear Function: A function of the form \(y=mx+b\) where \(m\) and \(b\) are constants, and \(m\) is the slope. Its graph is a straight line.

Minimums: The lowest point on the graph of a function. For quadratic functions with a positive leading coefficient, the vertex represents the minimum value.

Maximums: The highest point on the graph of a function. For quadratic functions with a negative leading coefficient, the vertex represents the maximum value.

Quadratic Function: A function of the form \(y=ax^2+bx+c\) where \(a\neq 0\). Its graph is a parabola that can open upwards or downwards.

Sequential: A term used to describe objects (like numbers or functions) that follow a specific order or sequence.

Slope: A measure of the steepness of a line, defined as the ratio of the vertical change to the horizontal change between two points on the line. For linear functions, the slope is the coefficient of \(x\).

Square Root Function: A function that represents the square root of \(x\), written as \(y=\sqrt{x} \)​. Its graph is a half-parabola that opens to the right.

Vertex: The highest or lowest point of a parabola, depending on its direction. The vertex of a quadratic function is a point where the function attains its maximum or minimum value.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore the different types of functions, their equations, and the graphs they create in the realm of Algebra 1. Understanding these fundamental function types is crucial as each has unique characteristics and applications. We'll dive into linear, quadratic, cubic, square root, cube root, absolute value, exponential growth, and exponential decay functions. For each function type, we'll discuss the parent function, its standard form (if available), the transformation form of equations, and the typical shape of their graphs. Additionally, we'll provide an example equation for each function type and explain why it fits into its respective category based on its equation and graph.

Function Types - Name, Equation, Graph

Function Type Parent Function Parent Graph Example
Linear

Parent Function:

\(y=x\)


Standard Form:

\(y=mx+b\)

where \(m\) is the slope, and \(b\) is the y-intercept.

Graph: The graph of a linear function is a straight line.

\(f(x)=-\frac{2}{3}x+3\)

This equation represents a linear function because it has a constant rate of change (slope) of \(-\frac{2}{3}\), meaning for each increase in \(x\), \(y\) decreases by \(\frac{2}{3}\). The graph of this function would be a straight line with a slope of \(-\frac{2}{3}\) and a y-intercept of 3.
Quadratic

Parent Function:

\(y=x^2\)


Standard Form:

\(y=ax^2+bx+c\)

where a, b, and c are constants and \(a\neq 0\)


Transformation Form:

\(y=a(x-h)^2+k\)

where h and k are constants that represent the vertex.

Graph: The graph of a quadratic function is a parabola, which can open upwards or downwards depending on the sign of a.

\(y=x^2-4x+3\) or \(y=(x-2)^2-1\) in vertex form

This equation is quadratic because it includes a term with \(x^2\), which is the defining characteristic of quadratic functions. The graph of this function would be a parabola opening upwards (since the coefficient of \(x^2\) is positive) with a vertex and an axis of symmetry.
Cubic

Parent Function:

\(y=x^3\)


Standard Form:

\(y=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\)


Transformation Form:

\(y=a(x-h)^3+k\)

Graph: An S-shaped curve that extends indefinitely in both directions.

\(y=x^3-4x^2+3x+1\)

It includes an \(x^3\) term, resulting in an S-shaped curve with a single inflection point.
Square Root

Parent Function:

\(y=\sqrt{x}\)


Transformation Form:

\(y=a\sqrt{x-h}+k\)

Graph: A half parabola that extends to the right from the vertex, increasing slowly.

\(y=2\sqrt{x-3}+1\)

With the square root sign, this function’s graph starts at \((3,1)\) and extends to the right, illustrating the characteristic shape of a square root function.

Based on the square root, its graph is a shifted half-parabola that increases gradually.
Cube Root

Parent Function:

\(y=\sqrt[3]{x}\)


Transformation Form:

\(y=a\sqrt[3]{x-h}+k\) 

Graph: An S-shaped curve similar to the cubic function but more symmetric around the origin.

\(y=\sqrt[3]{x+4}-5\)

Due to the cube root operation, its graph is S-shaped and symmetric, starting shifted to the left by 4 units and down 5 units.

It shows a cube root relationship, producing an S-shaped graph centered on a shifted point.
Absolute Value

Parent Function:

\(y=|x|\)


Transformation Form:

\(y=a|x-h|+k\)

Graph: A V-shaped graph with the point at \((h,k)\),  forming the vertex of the V.

\(y=2|x+1|-4\)

This function is recognized by the absolute value symbol, creating a V-shaped graph that is symmetric about the line \(x=-1\).
Exponential

Parent Function:

\(y=(2)^x\) or \(y=(\frac{1}{2})^x\)


Standard Form:

\(y=a(b)^x\)

where \(a\) is a constant, \(b\) is the base of the exponential function with \(b>0\) or \(0<b<1\), and \(b\neq 1\)

Graph: The graph of an exponential function can show exponential growth (if \(b>1\)) or exponential decay (if \(0<b<1\)).

\(y=2(\frac{1}{3})^x\)

This equation represents an exponential function because the variable \(x\) is in the exponent. The base of the exponential is \(\frac{1}{3}\), indicating that the function will show exponential decay. The graph of this function will rapidly decrease as \(x\) increases.


Conclusion

This lesson covered a broad spectrum of function types, including linear, quadratic, cubic, square root, cube root, absolute value, exponential growth, and exponential decay functions. By understanding the standard and transformation forms of these functions, as well as recognizing the distinct shapes of their graphs, you can better interpret and solve various algebraic problems. Each example provided illustrates why its respective function type fits its equation and graph, offering insight into how different functions behave. This foundational knowledge is vital for progressing in Algebra 1 and beyond, enabling you to apply mathematical concepts to both theoretical and real-world scenarios.
