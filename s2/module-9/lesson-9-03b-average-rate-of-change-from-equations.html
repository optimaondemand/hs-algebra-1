Average Rate of Change from Equations

📚 VOCABULARY

Average rate of change: The change in the value of a function's output divided by the change in the input over a specific interval.

Function: A relationship where each input has exactly one output.

Input: The value substituted into a function, often represented by \(x\).

Interval: A set of numbers between two endpoints, often describing the domain of inputs being considered.

Output: The result or value produced by a function after substituting an input, often represented by \(f(x)\) or \(y\).

Slope: A measure of the steepness of a line, found by dividing the change in \(y\) by the change in \(x\) between two points.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to calculate the average rate of change when you are given the equation of a nonlinear function and a specific interval. Unlike linear functions, where the rate of change remains constant, nonlinear functions have changing rates depending on which interval you are looking at. The average rate of change gives us a snapshot of how quickly the function's output is changing over the interval we are studying. By the end of this lesson, you will know how to substitute values into a function, find the corresponding outputs, and use them to calculate the average rate of change. We will also practice interpreting the meaning of the average rate of change in real-world contexts.


How to Find the Average Rate of Change from an Equation

When given a function \(f(x)\) and an interval \([a,b]\):

  1. Find the output at the start of the interval, \(f(a)\). Substitute the input into the function and evaluate.

  2. Find the output at the end of the interval, \(f(b)\).

  3. Apply the formula Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\).



Example 1

Find the average rate of change of the function \(f(x)=x^2+2x\) over the interval \([1,4]\).

Solution:

  • We begin by substituting in the first input of the interval into the function and evaluating it.
    • \(f(1)=(1)^2+2(1)\)
    • \(f(1)=1+2\)
    • \(f(1)=3\)
  • Next, we substitute in the second input for the end of the interval into the function and evaluate.
    • \(f(4)=(4)^2+2(4)\)
    • \(f(4)=16+8\)
    • \(f(4)=24\)
  • Now, we can use the average rate of change formula to calculate the average rate of change on the given interval.
  • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{24-3}{4-1}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{21}{3}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=7\)
  • The average rate of change for the function \(f(x)=x^2+2x\) over the interval \([1,4]\) is \(7\).



Example 2

A ball is thrown into the air. Its height \(h(t)\) in meters after \(t\) seconds is given by the function.

\(h(t)=-5t^2+20t\)

Find and interpret the average rate of change from \(t=1\) second to \(t=4\) seconds.

Solution:

  • We will begin by substituting the 1 into the function to find the height after 1 second.
    • \(h(1)=-5(1)^2+20(1)\)
    • \(h(1)=-5(1)+20(1)\)
    • \(h(1)=-5+20\)
    • \(h(1)=15\)
  • Next, we will substitute in 4 into the function to find the height after 4 seconds.
    • \(h(4)=-5(4)^2+20(4)\)
    • \(h(4)=-5(16)+20(4)\)
    • \(h(4)=-80+80\)
    • \(h(4)=0\)
  • Now we will use the formula to calculate the average rate of change.
  • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{15-0}{1-4}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{15}{-3}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=-5\)
  • The ball is falling at a rate of 5 meters per second.



Example 3

The distance \(d(t)\) in meters that a car travels while breaking is modeled by the function where \(t\) is the time in seconds after the brakes are applied.

\(d(t)=2t^2\)

Find and interpret the average rate of change from \(t=2\) seconds to \(t=5\) seconds.

Solution:

  • We will begin by substituting the 2 into the function to find the height after 2 seconds.
    • \(d(1)=2(2)^2\)
    • \(d(1)=2(4)\)
    • \(d(1)=8\)
  • Next, we will substitute in 5 into the function to find the height after 5 seconds.
    • \(d(5)=2(5)^2\)
    • \(d(5)=2(25)\)
    • \(d(5)=50\)
  • Now we will use the formula to calculate the average rate of change.
  • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{50-8}{5-2}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=\frac{42}{3}\)
    • Average Rate of Change \(=14\)
  • On average, the car traveled 14 meters per second between 2 and 5 seconds after applying the brakes.



Conclusion

Today we learned how to find the average rate of change when given an equation of a nonlinear function and an interval. By substituting the endpoints of the interval into the function and using the formula for average rate of change, we can calculate how quickly the function's output is changing. In real-world problems, interpreting this average rate helps us understand important information, such as how fast an object is moving or how far it is traveling. Remember, the average rate of change summarizes what happens over the entire interval and doesn't describe every moment individually, especially for nonlinear functions where the actual rate may vary!
