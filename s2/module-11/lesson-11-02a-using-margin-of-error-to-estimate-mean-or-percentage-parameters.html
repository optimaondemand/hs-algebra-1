📚 VOCABULARY

Confidence Interval: A range of values that is likely to contain the true population parameter. It is calculated from the sample data and gives an estimate of where the population parameter lies.

Confidence Level: The probability that the confidence interval contains the true population parameter. Common confidence levels are 90%, 95%, and 99%.

Estimation: The process of inferring the value of a population parameter based on sample data.

Margin of Error: The maximum expected difference between the sample statistic (like the mean or proportion) and the actual population parameter. It indicates the range within which the true value is expected to fall.

Parameter: A numerical characteristic of a population, such as a mean or proportion, that summarizes some aspect of the population.

Population: The entire group of individuals or items that you want to learn about in a study.

Population Mean: The average value of a numerical characteristic for the entire population.

Population Percentage: The proportion of the population that has a particular characteristic, expressed as a percentage.

Population Proportion: The fraction or decimal representation of the entire population that possesses a specific characteristic, found by dividing the number of individuals with the characteristic by the total population.

Population Total: The total number of individuals or items in the population.

Random Sampling: A method of selecting a sample from a population in such a way that every possible sample has an equal chance of being chosen.

Sample: A subset of individuals or items selected from the larger population, used to make inferences about the population.

Sample Proportion: The proportion of the sample that has a particular characteristic. It is calculated by dividing the number of items with the characteristic by the total sample size.

Sample Size: The number of individuals or items included in the sample.

Sample Survey: A study that collects data from a sample of the population to learn about the entire population.

Statistical Inference: The process of using data from a sample to make estimates or test hypotheses about the population.

Survey Data: The information collected from individuals in a sample survey.

Variability: The extent to which data points in a statistical distribution or data set differ from each other. High variability means data points are spread out; low variability means they are close together.

Specifically, we will focus on using the margin of error to determine the range within which the actual population mean or percentage likely falls. What is Margin of Error?

Margin of error (MOE) is a measure of the uncertainty or potential error in an estimate derived from a sample. It represents the range within which we expect the true population parameter (mean or percentage) to fall. The margin of error is typically expressed as a percentage or a numerical value and is used alongside the sample statistic (mean or proportion) to create an interval estimate.
