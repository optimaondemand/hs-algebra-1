📚 VOCABULARY

Area Model: An area model is a visual tool often used to simplify multiplication problems, particularly those involving multi-digit numbers or algebraic expressions. This model is typically a rectangle, with the length and width representing the factors, and the area representing the product.

Binomial: A binomial is an algebraic expression that has exactly two terms. These terms are separated by either a plus or minus sign. For example, \(3x+2\) is a binomial.

Degree: The degree of a polynomial is the highest power of the variable in that polynomial. For instance, in the polynomial \(2x^3-5x^2+4x+7\), the degree is \(3\) because the highest power of the variable \(x\) is \(3\).

Expression: An expression is a mathematical phrase that can include numbers, variables, operation symbols, and grouping symbols. For example, \(2x^2+3x-2\) is an expression.

Factoring: Factoring is the process of breaking down a number, an expression, or a polynomial into its smallest components. The factors of the polynomial \(x^2-4\) are \((x+2)\) and \((x-2)\).

Leading Coefficient: The leading coefficient is the numerical factor of the term in a polynomial that contains the highest power of the variable. For instance, in the polynomial \(-3x^4+5x^2-2\), the leading coefficient is \(-3\).

Monomial: A monomial is an algebraic expression with only one term. It can be a single number (like 5), a variable (like x), or a number multiplied by a variable (like 5x).

Polynomial: A polynomial is a type of mathematical expression that can have constants, variables, and exponents, which can be combined using addition, subtraction, and multiplication. It's important to note that in a polynomial, variables' exponents must be non-negative integers. Examples include \(2x^3-5x^2+4x+7\).

Standard Form of a Polynomial: The standard form of a polynomial arranges its terms by degree in descending order. The term with the highest degree is written first, followed by the term with the next highest degree, and so on until the term with the lowest degree (which is often the constant term). For example, the polynomial \(2x^3-5x^2+4x+7\) is in standard form.

Term: A term in algebra can be a single number (also known as a constant), a variable, or numbers and variables multiplied together. In the polynomial \(2x^3-5x^2+4x+7\), the terms are \(2x^3\), \(-5x^2\), \(4x\), and \(-7\).

Trinomial: A trinomial is an algebraic expression with three terms. For example, \(x^2+5x+4\) is a trinomial.

Now that we have explored creating a model using algebra tiles of a trinomial to determine the two binomial factors and explored factoring trinomials using area models and grouping, we will review a couple examples to sum up what we have learned.

Let's first look at factoring a trinomial using an area model.

Example 1

Factor \(3x^2-2x-8\).

Solution:

For this example, we began with multiplying the leading coefficient 3 and the constant \(-8\) to get the number \(-24\). We can write out the factor pairs for \(-24\) and find which pair sums to the coefficient of the middle term, \(-2\).

Notice that we found \(-6 \cdot 4=-24\) and \(-6+4=-2\).

So, we will use the two numbers \(-6\) and \(4\) to split the middle term. You can think of rewriting the original trinomial as the equivalent expression \(3x^2-6x+4x-8\).

This gives us the \(4\) terms to fill in our area model. Now, we look for the GCF in each row and in each column to find the column headers and row headers which you see written in purple.

Once we identify those GCF's and fill them in, we can now write the two binomial factors of the given trinomial; \(3x^2-2x-8=(x-2)(3x+4)\).

To help you see how we determined the \(-6\) and \(4\) as the coefficients of \(x\) to split the middle term into, here is a factor sum table to show the process of determining the pair of numbers that works.

To begin the table for other trinomials, you must multiply the leading coefficient and constant first.

Factors of -24 Sum of Factor Pairs

Now, let's look at another example of factoring a trinomial using grouping.

Example 2

Factor \(10x^2+17x+3\).

Solution:

As stated above, you can make a factor/sum table if needed to assist in finding the factor pair that works.

For this example, we begin with multiplying the leading coefficient \(10\) and the constant \(3\) to get the number \(30\). We will now think about factors of 30 that will sum to the middle term's coefficient of \(17\). We notice that \(2\cdot 15=30\) and \(2+15=17\). So, we will use \(2\) and \(15\) as coefficients of \(x\) to split the middle term into two terms \(2x\) and \(15x\).

We rewrite the trinomial as the equivalent expression \(10x^2+17x+3\). To use factor-by-grouping, we group the first two terms \((10x^2+2x)\) and the last two terms \((15x+3)\).

Next, we look for the GCF in each of the groupings. In the first group, ((10x^2+2x)\), we notice that both terms have a \(2x\) in them. So, we factor out \(2x\) and we get \(2x(5x+1)\). Keep in mind that when you divide \(2x\) by \(2x\), you get a \(1\), not zero. Therefore, we have \(a+1\) in place of the \(2x\) when we factor out \(2x\).

In the second group, \((15x+3)\), we notice that both terms have a \(3\) in them. So, we factor out a \(3\) and get \(3(5x+1)\). Now, that the two groups are identical, we factor the group out and join the GCF's in their own group to create the two binomial factors.

The final answer is \((2x+3)(5x+1)\).

Now, let's look at one final example where the leading coefficient is a negative number.

Example 3

Factor \(-6x^2+5x+21\).

In this example, we begin by multiplying the leading coefficient \(-6\) and the constant \(21\) to get \(-126\).

We look for factors of \(-126\) that will sum to the coefficient of the middle term, \(5\). Notice that \(-9\cdot 14=-126\) and \(-9+14=5\).

So, we can split the middle term \(5x\) into \(-9x\) and \(14x\). In this example, we used the factor-by-grouping method to group the first two terms, \((-6x^2-9x)\), and the last two terms, \((14x+21)\).

Now, we look for the GCF of each grouping. In the first group, we can factor out a \(-3x\) to create \(-3x(2x+3)\). In the second group, we can factor out a \(7\) creating \(7(2x+3)\). Now that what is left in the groups is identical, we can factor out this group and put the two GCF's into a group to create the two binomial factors.

The final answer is \((-3x+7)(2x+3)\).
