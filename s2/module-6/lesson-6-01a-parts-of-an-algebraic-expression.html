📚 VOCABULARY

Binomial: A binomial is a polynomial expression with exactly two terms. For example, \(3x + 2\) is a binomial, as is \(43z^2-37y\).

Coefficient: The coefficient is the numerical part of a term that includes a variable. For instance, in \(7x^2\), the coefficient is \(7\).

Constant: A constant is a term without a variable. Its value does not change, hence the name. In the binomial \(3x + 2\), the number \(2\) is a constant.

Exponent:  A number that shows how many times a base or a term is to be multiplied by itself. It is written as a small number to the upper right of the base or variable.

Exponential Function: An exponential function is a mathematical function of the form \(f(x)=a\cdot b^x\), where \(a\) is a constant, \(b\) is the base (a positive real number not equal to 0 or 1), and x is the exponent, which is a variable. This function shows growth or decay at a constantly increasing rate.

Expression: In algebra, an expression is a combination of numbers, variables, and operations (such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division). For instance, \(0.5x^2+7y-3\) is an expression.

Factor: When we talk about factoring a polynomial, we're trying to find what expressions we can multiply together to generate that polynomial. For instance, the factors of the polynomial \(x^2-x-6\) are \((x-3)\) and \((x+2)\).

Linear Function: A linear function is a function that produces a straight line when graphed. It can be represented in the form \(f(x)=mx+b\), where \(m\) is the slope of the line and \(b\) is the y-intercept (the point where the line crosses the y-axis). The slope m represents the rate of change.

Monomial: A monomial is a polynomial with just one term. It can be a single number (like \(5\)), a variable (like \(x\)), a number multiplied by a variable (like \(7x\)), or a number multiplied by variables (like \(3x^2y\)).

Polynomial: A polynomial is an algebraic expression consisting of variables and coefficients, involving operations of addition, subtraction, multiplication, and whole number exponents of variables. It can have one or more terms. Examples include \(7h^3\) (a monomial), \(4t^2-3r\) (a binomial), and \(x^2y+7x-31y\) (a trinomial).

Quadratic Function: A quadratic function is a second-degree polynomial function of the form \(f(x)=ax^2+bx+c\), where \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) are real numbers, and a is not zero. The graph of the quadratic function is a parabola, which can either open upwards or downwards depending on the sign of \(a\). 

Term: In algebra, a term can be a single number, a variable, or numbers and variables multiplied together. For instance, in the expression, \(3x+2\), \(3x\) and \(2\) are both terms.

Trinomial: A trinomial is a polynomial with exactly three terms. For example, \(x^2y+7x-31y\) is a trinomial.

Variable: In mathematics, a variable is a symbol, usually a letter, that represents an unspecified or unknown number.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

For today's lesson, we will be learning about some very important mathematical vocabulary words and what a mathematical representation of each of those words represents. Review the vocabulary words above to familiarize yourself.

When you have a basic understanding of the vocabulary words, watch the video.


Let's look at an expression and describe the expression and parts of the expression using the vocabulary words.

\(-19xy^3+15xy-21\)

The expression shown is a trinomial because it has three terms. The three terms are listed below:

\(-19xy^3\)

\(15xy\)

\(-21\)

The coefficients are \(-19\) and \(15\).

There are two variables in the expression, \(x\) and \(y\).

The constant in the expression is \(-21\).


We can describe polynomial expressions by how many terms the expression has. Remember that terms are separated by addition or subtraction. A single term can have numbers and variables using multiplication or division.

  • One term is called a Monomial
    • Example: \(\frac{5}{2}x^3y^2\)
  • Two terms is called a Binomial
    • Example: \(3x^4-\frac{1}{2}y\)
  • Three terms is called a Trinomial
    • \(-5xy+6.2x^2-8x^2y^2\)


We can describe algebraic functions by the type of function they are. Let's take a look at the three different types of functions we will continue to explore throughout the course.

  • Linear Functions - A linear function is a function that produces a straight line when graphed. A linear function can have 1 or 2 terms and has a constant rate of change.
    • Examples: \(f(x)=\frac{3}{4}x-8.7\) or \(7y-9x=15\)
  • Quadratic Functions - A second-degree polynomial function that produces a parabola or U-shaped graph. A second-degree polynomial indicates that one of the terms will have a power of 2. A quadratic function can have 1, 2, or 3 terms provided one of the terms has a power of 2 on the variable.
    • Examples: \(f(x)=-4x^2+7\) or \(y=x^2-7x+14\) or \(y=\frac{5}{2}x^2\)
  • Exponential Functions - A function that has a constant rate of growth or decay and can have 1 or 2 terms where one of the terms has a base, b, that has a variable as an exponent.
    • Examples: \(y=5^x+7\) or \(f(x)=-7(8)^x\)

 

Some of the vocabulary words like "factor" will be important in later lessons within this module and future modules. All of the words will be used in the lessons throughout the course.
