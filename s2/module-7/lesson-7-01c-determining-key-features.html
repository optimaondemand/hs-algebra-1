📚 VOCABULARY

Axis of Symmetry: A vertical line that divides a parabola into two mirror images, passing through its vertex. For a quadratic function \(y=ax^2+bx+c\), the axis of symmetry is \(x=-\frac{b}{2a}\).

Coefficient: A number used to multiply a variable. For example, in \(2x\), 2 is the coefficient of \(x\).

Completing the Square: A method used to convert a quadratic equation from standard form to vertex form, making it easier to graph and solve.

Coordinate Pair: A pair of numbers \((x,y)\) that indicate the position of a point on a coordinate plane.

Description: An explanation or representation of a mathematical concept or function that helps in understanding its properties or behaviors.

Factoring: The process of breaking down an equation into simpler components (factors) that, when multiplied together, produce the original equation.

Interpretation: The process of explaining the meaning of a mathematical result or graph within a specific context.

Maximum: The highest point on the graph of a function. In a quadratic function that opens downward, the vertex represents the maximum value.

Minimum: The lowest point on the graph of a function. In a quadratic function that opens upward, the vertex represents the minimum value.

Parabola: The shape of the graph of a quadratic function, characterized by being symmetrical and either opening upwards or downwards.

Plotting: The act of drawing a graph or marking points on a coordinate plane based on given values or equations.

Quadratic Function: A type of polynomial function that can be written in the form \(y=ax^2+bx+c\), where a, b, and c are constants and . Its graph is a parabola.

Standard Form: The way of writing a quadratic function as \(y=ax^2+bx+c\).

Table of Values: A set of pairs of numbers that represent the \(x\) and \(y\) values of a function, often used to help graph the function.

Vertex: The highest or lowest point of a parabola, depending on whether it opens upwards or downwards, representing the point of maximum or minimum value of a quadratic function.

Vertex Form: A way of writing a quadratic function as \(y=a(x-h)^2+k\), where \((h,k)\) is the vertex of the parabola.

X-intercepts: The points where the graph of a function crosses the x-axis. For a quadratic function, these are the values of \(x\) for which \(y=0\).

Y-intercepts: The point where the graph of a function crosses the y-axis. This occurs when \(x=0\).

Zero Product Property: A property stating that if the product of two numbers is zero, then at least one of the numbers must be zero. It is often used in factoring quadratic equations to find their zeros (or roots).

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will be exploring quadratic functions, their graphs, and the key features of the function. We will observe how we can use the graph to help identify key features or how to use the function to find key features that are not easily accessible from the graph. Quadratic functions are an essential concept in Algebra 1, characterized by an equation. The graph of a quadratic function is a parabola. Understanding the key features of this parabola is crucial for analyzing and graphing quadratic functions effectively. Let's explore these features: the vertex, axis of symmetry, zeros, y-intercept, domain, and range.


We will begin by reading about the key features of a quadratic function.

Vertex

The vertex is the highest or lowest point of the parabola, depending on whether it opens downwards or upwards, respectively. It's the point where the function changes direction, represented by \((h,k)\).

Axis of Symmetry

This is a vertical line that passes through the vertex of the parabola and divides it into two mirror-image halves. The axis of symmetry's equation is \(x=h\), where h is the x-coordinate of the vertex.

Zeros

Also known as x-intercepts or roots, the zeros of a quadratic function are the x-values where the function equals zero. These are the points where the parabola crosses the x-axis.

Y-intercept

The y-intercept is the point where the parabola crosses the y-axis, determined by setting \(x=0\) in the quadratic equation. The y-intercept has coordinates \((0,c)\) in the standard form \(y=a(x-h)^2+k\).

Domain and Range

  • Domain: The set of all possible x-values. For any quadratic function, the domain is all real numbers \((-\infty, \infty)\) or \(-\infty < x< \infty\).
  • Range: The set of possible y-values. The range is determined by the vertex. If the parabola opens upwards, the range is \(y\geq k\), and if it opens downwards, the range is \(y\leq k\), where k is the y-coordinate of the vertex.

Example 1

The equation representing a quadratic function is given, and its graph is shown.

\(y=x^2-x-2\)

Identify the key features of the quadratic function.

  • We will begin with the vertex. You will notice that the vertex is not exact and cannot easily be determined using the graph. So, instead, we will use the equation and the formula for the axis of symmetry.
  • We begin with finding the axis of symmetry using the formula \(x=-\frac{b}{2a}\), where a and b come from the equation \(y=x^2-x-2\); \(b=-1\) and \(a=1\).
    • \(x=\frac{-1}{2(1)}=\frac{1}{2}\)
  • We find that the Axis of Symmetry is \(x=\frac{1}{2}\) or \(x=0.5\).
  • This also represents the x-coordinate of the vertex. To find the y-coordinate, we substitute the value of x into the equation and evaluate for the value of y.
  • We find that the vertex of the quadratic function is \((\frac{1}{2},-\frac{9}{4})\).
  • Next, we can identify the zeros or the x-intercepts of the function.
    • We can identify these from the graph. We see that the graph crosses the x-axis at \(-1\) and \(2\).
    • We must write the zeros as ordered pairs since they represent a point. We also write them as a set using set brackets.
  • We find that the zeros of the function are \(\{(-1,0),(2,0)\}\).
  • Next, we will find the y-intercept of the function. We can use the equation and the value of c to identify the y-intercept as \((0,c)\). We also notice that we can see the graph crosses the y-axis at the value \(-2\). We must write the y-intercept as an ordered pair since it represents a point.
  • We find that the y-intercept is \((0,-2)\).
  • Last, we will identify the domain and range of the function.
    • The domain of any quadratic function is always all real numbers. We write this as an inequality \(-\infty < x< \infty\).
    • The range always starts with the y value of the vertex.
      • If the parabola opens upwards, then it will be greater than or equal to the y value of the vertex or k.
    • Since our parabola opens upwards and the y value of the vertex is \(-\frac{9}{4}\), then our range is all values greater than or equal to \(-\frac{9}{4}\). We write this as an inequality \(y\geq -\frac{9}{4}\).
  • We find that the Domain = \(-\infty < x< \infty\) and the Range = \(y\geq -\frac{9}{4}\).

Conclusion

Understanding the key features of quadratic functions allows students to grasp the essential aspects of their graphs fully. The vertex, axis of symmetry, zeros, y-intercept, domain, and range each tell a part of the story of the function's behavior. Through practice with both the equations and graphs of quadratic functions, students can develop a deep and intuitive understanding of these fundamental components, enabling them to analyze and graph quadratic functions with confidence.
